AI Control Plane Assessment

Prepared by [name, title] for [client name]

How to use this template

This template is the written companion to the AI control plane assessment readout. It follows the same storyline as Part 2 of the client deck, in twelve sections and five appendices. Fill it from templates T1 to T13 in the guide Assessing an AI Control Plane Project. Part 3 of the guide shows every template filled in for reference use case 1 and is the model answer.

Replace every [bracketed prompt]. Paragraphs styled like this one are guidance: leave them out of the report you send, including this page. Search your report for “[” to find any prompt left.

Replace each figure placeholder with an image of the matching slide from Part 2 of the client deck, so the report and the readout match.

Describe each product capability by what it does, from public information, and label custom policies built with the Policy Development Kit as custom. Label every figure illustrative until the baseline is measured and finance agrees the method, and show a formula with every value.

Cite sources with superscript numbers from the shared endnote list in The AI Control Plane Handbook, and list only the sources you cite in Appendix D, with the same numbers.

Use these names. MuleSoft Agent Fabric is the control plane, and MuleSoft APIs, connectors and MCP Bridge are the capability layer. Informatica is the data foundation, and the runtime is CloudHub (MuleSoft-managed) or Runtime Fabric (customer-managed).

Before sending, run the quality checklist in Section 10 of the guide, and fill in your report’s cover, header, footer and document properties (title and subject).

The toolkit’s license, its additional permissions and the full notices are in Appendix E of the guide, and a note about the author is in Appendix F.

6. Security and compliance

Fill from T5. Give one or two lines of controls per layer and what enforces each, the threats the design covers with the owner of each gap, and the evidence each obligation requires. For every agent, show its tier, the evidence to promote it, what demotes it and who takes over its work. It matches slide 35.

Security follows zero-trust principles: every actor is authenticated, every call is authorized for the specific task, and nothing is trusted because of where it sits on the network.90 Every agent has an autonomy tier that sets how much it may do on its own, and every write to a system of record needs an approval bound to the exact action, or a person. Agents move up a tier only on evidence and drop a tier automatically on defined triggers, and every agent at tier 3 or above has a takeover runbook.
