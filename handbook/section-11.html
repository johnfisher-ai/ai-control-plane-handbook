Section 11

Reference use case 1: Professional services engagement launch

This reference use case applies the control-plane architecture to one of the most frequent, compliance-sensitive processes in a global audit, tax and advisory firm: accepting and launching a new client engagement. The architecture and discussion are vendor-neutral except for the transactional systems the process runs on: a CRM, SAP S/4HANA for projects and billing, Workday for people and skills, and Snowflake for analytics. The final subsection shows one way to realize the design with MuleSoft Agent Fabric and Informatica.

Overview

Large professional services firms launch thousands of engagements a year. Between a verbal win and the first chargeable hour sits a chain of manual, risk-sensitive steps: resolving exactly which legal entity the client is, checking conflicts of interest and auditor independence, drafting the engagement letter, building the budget, staffing the team, and creating the project, work breakdown structure (WBS) and billing plan in the ERP system. Each step lives in a different system and is owned by a different function, so the chain commonly takes days to weeks. The delays have direct costs. People wait on the bench, and work that starts before charge codes exist goes unbilled. A missed affiliate relationship can also become an independence breach with regulatory consequences when the client, or its parent, is an audit client.89

In the target state, an Engagement Launch Orchestrator coordinates six specialist agents across these systems. People keep every judgment that carries professional or regulatory accountability: independence decisions, engagement terms and partner approval. Agents do the assembly, checking, drafting and system updates, and the control plane makes every step governed, observable and auditable.

  • Trigger: an opportunity reaches verbal win in the CRM.
  • End state: independence cleared and documented, engagement letter signed, team soft-booked, and the SAP project, WBS, billing plan and charge codes active.
  • Users: engagement managers and partners, Risk & Quality (independence reviewers), resource management and finance.
  • Out of scope: delivery of the engagement itself and invoicing after launch.

Solution architecture

The architecture follows the five-layer reference architecture in Section 6, expanded to show the orchestration model, the MCP and A2A paths, the data-governance layer and the security controls that apply at every layer (Figure 9).

Clients and channels

Engagement managers use a chat copilot inside the firm’s collaboration tool, and partners approve from their phones with step-up authentication. Independence reviewers stay in their case workspace, while clients sign through an e-signature portal. Every request enters through the orchestrator and the control plane, so no user talks to a model directly.

Orchestration model selected

The firm uses the hybrid/federated pattern, in which the control plane centrally governs identity, policy, cost and audit and each function owns its agents. Risk & Quality owns the Conflicts & Independence agent and its rules, Finance owns Engagement Setup, and Talent owns Staffing. Within a launch, the orchestrator acts as supervisor, decomposing the launch into tasks and delegating them to specialist agents over A2A,27 and agents reach tools and data only through MCP servers.26 The regulated sequence is deterministic. No letter is sent before independence is cleared, and nothing is written to SAP before the partner and the client have approved. Model reasoning works inside the steps (resolving, summarizing, drafting, matching) and can never skip a gate.

Models and routing

  • Frontier model (primary): hosted under enterprise terms with zero data retention, it drafts engagement letters and summarizes complex findings. A second frontier provider is configured for failover.
  • Open-weight small language model: self-hosted in the firm’s virtual private cloud for entity extraction, classification and personal-data detection, so client-confidential text never leaves the firm for routine tasks.
  • Embedding model: on a private endpoint to index credentials, prior proposals and templates for retrieval.
  • Routing policy in the AI gateway: client-confidential payloads go only to private models or approved regions, and drafting goes to the frontier model with personal data masked. Each launch has its own token budget.

Control plane and policy

The control plane provides these services:

  • A registry of agents, MCP servers and APIs, each with an owner and an autonomy tier.
  • An AI gateway that mediates all LLM, MCP and A2A traffic.
  • A policy decision point that evaluates policy-as-code.
  • Identity services that issue per-agent workload identities and delegated, on-behalf-of tokens.
  • Evaluation, cost and observability services, and a kill switch with circuit breakers.

Representative policies for this use case:

  • Engagement setup in SAP requires independence status CLEARED, partner approval and a signed letter. The partner’s approval is bound to the exact setup request and expires at the end of the business day.
  • Engagement letters use approved templates, and any clause deviation routes to Risk & Quality.
  • Staffing proposals include only people with independence clearance for the client and the required credentials.
  • Client-confidential data is processed only by private models in approved regions.

MCP servers, APIs and systems of record

Each system of record is wrapped by governed APIs and exposed to agents as MCP tools with explicit read or write scopes: the CRM (opportunity and account), the independence and conflicts system (searches and a clearance flag), document management and e-signature, Workday (skills, certifications, availability), SAP S/4HANA (rate cards, projects, WBS, billing plans) and Snowflake (historical engagement economics through governed semantic views, read-only).

Master data and semantics

Independence and conflict checks depend on the entity data behind them, because a missed subsidiary means a missed conflict. Master data management therefore sits on the critical path:

  • Client and legal-entity master: one golden record per legal entity with the full corporate hierarchy (ultimate parent, affiliates, subsidiaries), identifiers such as the LEI and tax IDs, and links to the restricted-entity list.
  • Worker master: Workday remains the system of record, and MDM cross-references worker identifiers across the CRM, SAP and the independence system.
  • Engagement master: one engagement ID shared by the CRM opportunity, the independence case, the engagement letter and the SAP project.
  • Reference data: service catalog, service lines, industries and rate cards, with effective dates.

A semantic layer gives agents and analytics the same meaning for the same words: a business glossary (client, affiliate, audit client, restricted entity, engagement, realization, utilization), shared metric definitions, and a data contract for each MCP tool (schema, meaning and quality thresholds). A catalog records lineage from source to agent output, so a reviewer can see which records drove an independence finding. Data-quality rules watch hierarchy freshness, duplicate entities and missing identifiers, and access policies enforce classification, masking and information barriers between engagements.

Security at every level

Security follows zero-trust principles,90 so every actor is authenticated and every call is authorized for its specific task, and nothing is trusted because of where it sits on the network.

Governance and observability

Risk & Quality owns the independence agent’s rules and approves changes to them, while an AI governance council approves autonomy tiers and new agents. Before promotion, every agent passes evaluation suites for letter clause accuracy, independence-finding recall and entity-resolution precision. Every hop emits OpenTelemetry traces using the generative-AI semantic conventions,94 tied to one engagement correlation ID, so operations can see cycle time by stage, policy blocks, token cost per launch and evaluation drift on one dashboard.

Maya’s day as an engagement manager

Maya Chen is an engagement manager in the advisory practice. The timeline follows an illustrative launch for a new client, Project Atlas (Figure 10).

At 08:05 the CRM opportunity for Project Atlas moves to verbal win, and within a minute Maya’s chat copilot shows a launch plan targeting ready-to-charge in two days. By 08:12 the entity-resolution agent has matched the client to its golden record and found that it is a subsidiary of a group whose parent is an audit client, so an enhanced independence review starts automatically. At 09:40 a reviewer in Risk & Quality approves the engagement with one condition: no work on financial-reporting systems.

The scoping agent drafts the engagement letter with that carve-out by 10:30, and Maya rewrites two paragraphs. The pricing agent’s budget arrives at 11:20 with a 31% margin against a 30% target. At 13:05 the staffing agent proposes a team of five, having already replaced a consultant who is not independence-cleared for this client. Maya sees only that a substitution was required, not why. The partner approves the letter and budget on mobile at 14:30 with step-up authentication, and the letter goes to the client for signature. At 16:10 the client signs, the setup agent creates the SAP project, WBS and billing plan, and the team receives charge codes. The whole launch, which used to take about two weeks, finished the same day.
