About this handbook

This handbook sets out what an enterprise AI control plane is, why it has become the next infrastructure advantage, how to architect one, the vendor-neutral target reference architecture to steer by, one way to realize it with MuleSoft Agent Fabric and Informatica, and how to sequence the work over the next 12 to 24 months so that every milestone brings a strategic win.

Sections 11 to 13 apply the architecture to three reference use cases: a professional services firm, a capital markets firm and a natural gas pipeline operator.

It is written for CTOs, CIOs and the architects who work with them. Each chapter combines the architectural framework with the research behind it, and every source is listed in the endnotes.

In brief

  1. The new infrastructure advantage

    Advantage will come from the governed system that decides which model runs each task and under which constraints, rather than from any single model.

  2. Three capabilities

    Architecture exposes what the enterprise can do as secure, governed services. Orchestration coordinates models, agents, workloads and the decisions they make, and trust, security and resilience controls verify every actor and output continuously.

  3. Paradigm before platform

    Embedded, dedicated and hybrid/federated patterns each carry trade-offs, and most large multi-vendor enterprises end up with central policy and distributed execution.

  4. MuleSoft and Agent Fabric

    MuleSoft’s API-led connectivity exposes enterprise applications as governed APIs and MCP tools, and MuleSoft Agent Fabric discovers, governs, orchestrates and meters agents from any vendor.

  5. Value in every phase

    Over the next 12 to 24 months, each phase puts a slice of a real use case into production. The work covers an inventory of agents, one gateway for all model and tool traffic, autonomy tiers and agent identities, with post-quantum migration started ahead of the 2030–2031 federal deadlines.

About John Fisher

I’m John Fisher. At Salesforce, I help enterprises architect the data foundation that AI depends on: MuleSoft for integration and APIs, Informatica for data management, and the MCP and A2A protocols that connect agents to tools, data and each other. Before Salesforce, I spent fourteen years at TIBCO leading solution engineering for enterprise accounts. Across both companies, my work has sat where enterprise architecture, integration and data meet business strategy.

My writing is where I work through what happens when AI agents move past experiments and start acting across real enterprise systems. The questions that interest me most are architectural: how an agent proves who it is, what policy decides what it may reach, how its cost is measured and controlled, what evidence it leaves behind, how agents built on different platforms work together, and what it takes to run all of that at scale.

Some of what I write names Salesforce, MuleSoft or Informatica technology. It’s the technology I know most deeply, so it’s often the quickest way to make an idea concrete. I use it as one example of a broader pattern, not as the only answer, and the target architectures I start from are vendor-neutral by design.
